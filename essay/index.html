Essay Kit

Every essay is a case in court. The thesis is the charge, the quotes are the evidence, and the explaining is where you win it. Here is the build Mr. Winter is marking, shown on stories that aren't Macbeth. About 7 minutes.

Tap "Got it" at the end of each section. It saves on this phone.

01

The shapeFour paragraphs

The Macbeth essay is a 4-paragraph persuasive essay. You pick one of three questions and argue your answer.

  1. Introduction · ends with your thesis.
  2. Body 1 · your first argument, with two PQEs.
  3. Body 2 · your second argument, with two PQEs.
  4. Conclusion · thesis again in new words, then the bigger point.

After that: a Works Cited page.

Your turn

The assignment says the ideas have to be yours. So this page teaches the shape using other stories. Which question you pick, your thesis and your quotes are your call.

02

ThesisThe last sentence of your intro

A thesis answers the essay question in one sentence. Mr. Winter's formula:

Main point + two arguments that prove it

With Star WarsAnakin Skywalker falls because of fear: first the fear of losing his mother, then the fear of losing Padmé.

Main point: fear causes his fall. Argument one becomes body paragraph 1. Argument two becomes body paragraph 2.

Not a thesis

  • A topic: "Fear."
  • A fact: "Star Wars is a film series."
  • An announcement: "This essay will be about Anakin."

The rubric's top levels want a thesis that is clear, answers the question, and is specific.

03

PQEHow a body paragraph is built

P · PointA claim that proves your topic sentence.
Q · QuoteEvidence from the text, with a citation.
E · ExplainOne or two sentences on how the quote proves the point.

One body paragraph, about ten sentences

  1. Topic sentence · the argument this paragraph proves.
  2. Point 1
  3. Quote 1, embedded and cited.
  4. Explain 1 · one or two sentences.
  5. Point 2, Quote 2, Explain 2
  6. Closing sentence · link back to the thesis.
From the rubricTwo PQEs in each body paragraph is Level 4. One is Level 2. The E is where the thinking marks are.
04

QuotesSmall but mighty

  • Aim for 5 to 25 words.
  • Pick quotes with deeper meaning: figurative language beats plain plot.
  • Embed them: blend the quote into your own sentence. A quote never stands as a sentence on its own.
  • Skip "Shakespeare said, '…'". Mr. Winter asks for something more creative.

Square brackets

Use [ ] around any word you change so the sentence flows. You can change pronouns and verb tense.

The original line · The Rime"With my cross-bow / I shot the ALBATROSS."
EmbeddedThe Mariner gives no reason for his crime, admitting only that "with [his] cross-bow / [he] shot the ALBATROSS" (Coleridge 81–82).

Citing a play

For Shakespeare, the numbers are act.scene.lines. Mr. Winter's question sheet writes (1.7.71-74): Act 1, Scene 7, lines 71 to 74.

Write down act, scene and line numbers when you find each quote. Hunting for them later is slow.

05

Intro and conclusionThe bookends

Introduction, in order

  1. Hook · one or two sentences on the big topic. Don't name the play or the author yet.
  2. Connection · a transition into the work: title, author, genre.
  3. Summary · a brief summary; you can preview your body points.
  4. Thesis · the final sentence.
A hook for an Odyssey essayAlmost everyone has one place they would cross the world to get back to.

Conclusion, in order

  1. Transition · Ultimately, Therefore, Thus.
  2. Thesis, restated in different words.
  3. Recap · one sentence for each body point.
  4. What's the point? · a message about life that reaches past the play, maybe into today.

The handout's warning: don't write "The universal truth is…". Just say it.

06

Sound like an essayA quarter of the mark

Communication is 25 of the 100 marks. The rubric lists the rules:

  • No contractions. "does not," never "doesn't."
  • No personal pronouns. No I, me, my, you.
  • Literary present tense. The story happens now: Odysseus ties himself to the mast.
  • No slang or casual words.
Text-message voiceI think Odysseus tied himself up cuz he knew he'd fold.
Essay voiceOdysseus ties himself to the mast because he does not trust his own will.

Same idea both times. Only the second one earns the marks for it.

MLA, also 25 marks

  • Times New Roman 12, double-spaced.
  • In-text citation after every quote.
  • Works Cited page.
  • Rough work counts. Hand in the outline and the rough draft with the good copy.
07

Wrap-upThe seven steps

Mr. Winter's steps

  1. Answer the question. That's your thesis.
  2. Brainstorm how your two arguments prove it.
  3. Find quotes. Note act, scene and lines.
  4. Fill out the essay outline.
  5. Write the rough draft. Finish it; it doesn't have to be perfect.
  6. Bring it to class for editing.
  7. Polish and hand in.
