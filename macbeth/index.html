Macbeth

A soldier comes home from war a hero. Three strangers on a heath tell him he will be king. Five acts later, almost everyone he knows is dead. This is the map: who's who, what happens, and the five-step method Mr. Winter marks. About 8 minutes.

01

The playUnit 3 began Nov 10

Macbeth is a tragedy by William Shakespeare, written around 1606 and set in medieval Scotland. A war hero is told he will be king, and decides not to wait.

It is short, fast and violent. Almost every scene is about one question: what does wanting something badly do to a person?

Four words to own

  • Thane · a Scottish lord. Macbeth starts as Thane of Glamis.
  • Regicide · killing a king.
  • Soliloquy · a character alone, speaking their thoughts out loud. Nobody else on stage hears it.
  • Tragedy · a play that follows a great person's fall. The hero usually has a fatal flaw that drives it.
Mr. Winter's exampleIn his Hamlet model, Hamlet's fatal flaw is procrastination: he debates endlessly instead of acting.
02

Who's whoSeven names

  • Macbeth · a general and thane. Brave in battle, and tempted.
  • Lady Macbeth · his wife. She pushes the plan forward.
  • King Duncan · king of Scotland when the play opens. Trusts Macbeth.
  • Banquo · Macbeth's friend and fellow general. He hears the prophecy too. His son is Fleance.
  • Macduff · the thane who never trusts Macbeth, and faces him at the end.
  • Malcolm · Duncan's elder son and heir.
  • The three witches · the "weird sisters." They speak in riddles.
03

Five actsThe whole road

  1. Act 1 · The idea. Macbeth wins a battle. Three witches hail him as Thane of Glamis, Thane of Cawdor, and king "hereafter." Minutes later he is made Thane of Cawdor. Lady Macbeth reads his letter and starts planning. Duncan comes to stay at their castle.
  2. Act 2 · The deed. Macbeth sees a dagger in the air, then kills Duncan in his sleep. He carries the daggers out by mistake; Lady Macbeth takes them back. Macduff finds the body. Duncan's sons run: Malcolm to England, Donalbain to Ireland. Macbeth is crowned.
  3. Act 3 · The cover-up. The witches said Banquo's children would be kings, so Macbeth hires murderers. Banquo dies. Fleance escapes. That night Banquo's ghost sits in Macbeth's chair at the banquet.
  4. Act 4 · The spiral. Macbeth goes back to the witches and gets three new riddles. He has Macduff's wife and children killed. In England, Malcolm tests Macduff, then they raise an army.
  5. Act 5 · The fall. Lady Macbeth sleepwalks, scrubbing at her hands. She dies. The army cuts branches from Birnam Wood for cover, so the forest "moves." Macduff kills Macbeth. Malcolm is king.

The riddles from Act 4

  • Beware Macduff.
  • No one "of woman born" can harm Macbeth.
  • He is safe until Birnam Wood comes to Dunsinane.

Each one sounds like a promise. Watch how each one comes true in Act 5.

04

Five key scenesMr. Winter's list

The passage analysis handout ends with five scenes. Know where each one sits in the story.

  1. Act 1, Scene 3 · The witches' prophecy. Macbeth and Banquo meet the witches on the way back from battle.
  2. Act 1, Scene 7 · "Vaulting ambition." Macbeth, alone, argues with himself about killing Duncan. Then Lady Macbeth arrives.
  3. Act 3, Scene 4 · The ghost at the banquet. Macbeth is king. Banquo has just been killed. Only Macbeth sees the ghost.
  4. Act 4, Scene 1 · The witches, part two. Macbeth demands answers and gets the three riddles.
  5. Act 5, Scene 1 · "Out, damned spot!" Lady Macbeth sleepwalks while a doctor and a gentlewoman watch.
Your turn

What each speech means, and which motifs are in it, is the analysis itself. That part is yours. The next section shows the method on a different play.

05

Passage analysisFive steps, ten marks

  1. Speaker and listener · who is talking, and to whom? (1 mark)
  2. Context · act and scene, and what is happening at this point. (1)
  3. Summary · what the speaker is talking about, in your words. (2)
  4. Poetic devices · name them and point to the words. (2)
  5. Motifs and themes · which motifs appear, why they matter, and how the scene connects to the play's themes. (4)
Do the mathStep 5 is worth as much as steps 1, 2 and 3 added together. A long summary with a one-line step 5 leaves marks behind.

Watch it work · Romeo and Juliet

But soft, what light through yonder window breaks?
It is the east, and Juliet is the sun.

  1. The speaker is Romeo. He is speaking to himself; Juliet cannot hear him.
  2. Act 2, Scene 2. He has just met Juliet at her family's party and is hiding in their orchard, below her window.
  3. He sees a light at the window and says Juliet appearing is like the sun rising.
  4. Metaphor: "Juliet is the sun." No "like" or "as." Alliteration: "what … window."
  5. Motif: light against dark. The lovers keep meeting at night and describing each other as light. It ties to a theme of the play: their love is bright, sudden and surrounded by darkness.
06

Devices to spotStep 4 and step 5

Three sound devices

The handout names these three. They are finally the sound devices from the poetry unit.

  • Alliteration · the same sound at the start of nearby words. "Wild and whirling words."
  • Assonance · the same vowel sound inside nearby words. "The light of the fire is a sight."
  • Consonance · the same consonant sound anywhere in nearby words. "The lumpy, bumpy road."

Simile, metaphor, personification

SimileCompares with "like" or "as."
MetaphorSays one thing is another.

Personification gives a human action to something that isn't human: "Fear grabbed him."

The trap"Fear, like a cold hand, closed around him." The word "like" makes it a simile, even though a hand is human. Check for "like" or "as" first.

Motif

An image or idea that keeps coming back through a work. Each return adds to a theme.

Ones to watch for in Macbeth:

  • Blood
  • Sleep, and not being able to
  • Darkness and light
  • Hands and water
  • Clothes that don't fit
  • Nature behaving unnaturally
  • Visions and the supernatural

Spotting one is half of step 5. The other half is saying what it shows.
